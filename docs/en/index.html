A Feynman Technique coach that lives in your terminal

Turn "I thought I got it"
into "I know I got it"

Highlighting and re-reading create familiarity, not understanding. This skill makes AI play a zero-background but logically rigorous listener: you do the explaining, it does the probing — one why at a time, until you can explain the concept so clearly that an outsider could repeat it back.

$ npx skills add dull-bird/feynman-technique -g

Then say one line to your agent: "Use the Feynman Technique. The concept is compound interest."

Today's concept: compound interest — the one I'm most confident about

  1. You

    Compound interest is money making money — interest rolls into more interest.

  2. Listener

    What does "rolls" mean exactly? After year one, what actually landed in the account?

  3. You

    70 dollars... wait, year two earns 7% of 1070, not of 1000.

  4. Listener

    Then why would a bank hand you money for nothing?

  5. You

    (stuck) ...I need to go look that up.

The Method

A four-step loop, repeated until it flows

The order itself is information: each step catches the problems the previous one exposed.

1

Write down the concept

Tackle one concept at a time. Naming it is itself a commitment: for the next thirty minutes, your attention belongs to it alone.

The concept you're most confident about deserves the first test

2

Explain it to an outsider

Every jargon term is cheating; every skipped step is a hole. The bar isn't the listener nodding — it's the listener being able to repeat it back.

"That's obvious" = the part you haven't figured out

3

Spot the blind spots

Getting stuck isn't failure — it's a roadmap. Every "I don't actually get this" points precisely at what to learn next.

Blind spots are gold mines — don't gloss over them

4

Simplify with analogies

Delete every word that makes you sound smart but not clear. Would your mom get it? If not, rewrite.

Jargon is a crutch; mechanism is the leg

↺ After backfilling the blind spots, go back to step 2 and explain again — until the whole run is smooth.

Transcript

What a real round of probing looks like

The AI never explains for you — it only paraphrases and probes. One most-critical question at a time.

You

A marketing funnel is the conversion path that turns strangers into customers.

← jargon unexplained
Listener

What's a "conversion path"? Where does a stranger land on their very first step?

You

They see content first, then leave their contact info, then get persuaded to buy.

Listener

Why can't you skip the first two steps and sell to them directly?

← five-year-old test: answering "what" isn't enough
You

Because trust takes time — nobody hands money to someone they met three seconds ago.

← answered the "why" ✓

Architecture

Technical details

The AI owns conversation quality; the code owns process discipline.

You explain → AI listenerrubric in hand ⇄ Session state machinediscipline → JSONL log → Reports · Obsidian

State-machine driven

The process doesn't rely on prompt goodwill. Four commands — start / round / status / close — hard-code round caps, valid gap codes, and logging. Long conversations can't drift.

The examiner holds a rubric first

Before every session it researches the concept, decomposes it from first principles, writes 5–8 mastery checkpoints, and reads your history. Every probe has a target.

7-category gap taxonomy

Factual error > jargon dodge > causal gap > mechanism black box > boundary blur > broken analogy > edge-case blind. Factual errors always die first — they poison everything downstream.

Anti-parroting scaffold

Hints are capped at 60–100 words plus one example, and a scaffolded re-explanation counts as progress, not mastery — you must re-explain from a fresh angle.

Data loop

Every session lands in a JSONL log. Reports, the Obsidian export, and this site's gallery are all rebuilt from the same data.

Engineering backbone

18 pexpect end-to-end tests run on every commit, and a UserPromptSubmit hook auto-invokes the skill in Claude Code, Codex, and Kimi when you say "feynman".
